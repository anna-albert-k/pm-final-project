# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A heavy pay later user in their late 20s or early 30s who already has multiple pay later apps and uses them for most online purchases.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Pay later with no effort and always know what they owe, without adding another thing to check.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** They could not see in one place what they owed on the card and what they owed on regular pay later orders (N-07), and two reminders arrived in the same week for different amounts (T-104). This echoes N-02’s concern that a third product is “one more thing to check before payday.”
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Other pay later apps (unnamed), Bank or debit card, A second payment method at checkout
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** The 5 steps
Open the Riverty card screen and note the card balance.
Evidence: T-103
Status: Supported
Open the Riverty pay later orders screen and note that total, because it is not shown with the card balance.
Evidence: T-103, N-07
Status: Supported
Open the other pay later apps and note each balance. N-02 mentions two.
Evidence: N-02
Status: Partly supported. The apps exist, but how they are checked is not described.
Add the totals and match them against the reminders. Two reminders for different amounts landed in the same week, so they must work out which amount belongs to which product and date.
Evidence: T-104
Status: Hypothesis
Pay, or fall back to a familiar method if something looks wrong, for example a bank card after a decline or a late notification.
Evidence: N-06, T-108
Status: Supported
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** tep 2, when the person opens the pay later orders screen and sees a total that does not match the card balance, with two reminders in the same week asking for different amounts.

In plain terms: they are trying to answer “what do I owe before payday?” and the product gives them two numbers and two deadlines instead of one.

Why this moment, not the others
It is where the goal fails outright. The persona’s goal is to know what they owe without adding another thing to check. At this point they must check two places inside Riverty alone, and still cannot tell which total is real (T-103).
It is a Riverty-caused problem. Steps 3 and 5 involve other apps and the debit card. Step 2 is the one moment where our own product creates the confusion.
It is evidenced. T-103, T-104 and N-07 all point at it. The other candidate moments rest on hypothesis (Step 4) or on a different persona (the in-store decline in N-06).
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** N-07: “I could not see in one place what I owe on the card and what I owe on my normal pay later orders.”
T-103: Participants ask which total is the real one.
T-104: Statement date and pay later due dates differ, so two reminders for different amounts arrive in the same week.
N-02: A third product is “one more thing to check before payday.”
